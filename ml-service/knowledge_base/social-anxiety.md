# Social anxiety
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/social-anxiety/

## Overview
Social anxiety is a strong, lasting fear of social situations in which a person worries about being judged, embarrassed or humiliated. It is more than shyness and can make everyday things like talking in class, eating in front of others or making phone calls very difficult.

## How it can feel
People describe dreading social events for days, worrying they will say something stupid, feeling like everyone is watching or judging them, blushing, sweating or shaking in front of others, and replaying conversations afterwards and criticising themselves.

## Common signs
Intense fear of being judged; avoiding social situations, presentations or speaking up; worrying for a long time before events; physical symptoms such as blushing, sweating, trembling or a racing heart; going over conversations afterwards.

## What can help
Gradually facing feared situations in small steps rather than avoiding them. Noticing and questioning harsh thoughts about how others see you. Focusing attention outward on the conversation rather than on yourself. Slow breathing before and during difficult situations.

## When to get professional help
If fear of social situations is stopping you from studying, working or making friends, speak to a doctor or therapist. CBT is an effective treatment for social anxiety.
