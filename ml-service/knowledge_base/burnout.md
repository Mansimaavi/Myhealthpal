# Burnout
Source: https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2019-burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon-international-classification-of-diseases

## Overview
Burnout is described by the World Health Organization as an occupational phenomenon caused by long-term workplace stress that has not been managed well. It is not classified as a medical condition, but it has a real effect on health and wellbeing. Students can experience something similar from long periods of academic pressure.

## How it can feel
People describe feeling completely drained, running on empty, exhausted even after resting, and dreading work or studies. They may feel detached, cynical or like nothing they do matters, and notice they are less effective than they used to be.

## Common signs
Constant exhaustion; feeling negative, cynical or distant about work or studies; reduced performance; loss of motivation; trouble concentrating; frequent headaches or getting ill more often; poor sleep.

## What can help
Taking real breaks and protecting time off. Setting boundaries around working hours and messages. Talking to a manager, teacher or mentor about workload. Prioritising sleep, movement and time with people you care about. Reconnecting with activities that have nothing to do with work or studies.

## When to get professional help
Burnout can overlap with depression and anxiety. If exhaustion and low mood continue even after rest, or you are feeling hopeless, see a doctor or mental health professional.
