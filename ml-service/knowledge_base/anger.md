# Anger and irritability
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/anger/

## Overview
Anger is a normal emotion, but it becomes a problem when it is frequent, very intense, hard to control, or leads to hurting yourself, others or relationships. Irritability and anger can also be signs of stress, depression, anxiety or exhaustion.

## How it can feel
People describe snapping at people over small things, feeling constantly frustrated or annoyed, having a short fuse, feeling a rush of heat or tension in the body, and later regretting what they said or did.

## Common signs
Frequent irritability; outbursts of shouting or arguing; clenched jaw or fists, fast heartbeat; holding grudges; feeling misunderstood or treated unfairly; guilt after outbursts.

## What can help
Noticing early warning signs and taking a break before reacting. Slow breathing or counting before responding. Physical activity to release tension. Expressing feelings calmly once you have cooled down. Looking at what is underneath the anger, such as hurt, stress or tiredness.

## When to get professional help
If anger is damaging relationships, affecting work or studies, or you feel you might hurt someone or yourself, talk to a doctor or counsellor. Anger management and talking therapies can help.
