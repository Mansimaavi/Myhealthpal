# Loneliness
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/lifes-challenges/loneliness/

## Overview
Loneliness is the painful feeling of not having the connection or closeness with others that you want. It is not the same as being alone: people can feel lonely in a crowd, in a relationship or surrounded by family. Long-term loneliness is linked to a higher risk of depression and anxiety.

## How it can feel
People describe feeling isolated, left out, invisible, like nobody understands them or like they have no one to talk to. Moving to a new city, starting college, working from home or losing a relationship can all bring it on.

## Common signs
Feeling disconnected from others; lack of close relationships; spending a lot of time alone and wishing it was different; feeling that others would not care; low mood; increased time on social media while feeling worse.

## What can help
Reaching out to one person, even with a small message. Joining a group based on an interest, such as a class, club, sport or volunteering. Saying yes to invitations even when it feels hard. Being kind to yourself about how long it takes to build connections.

## When to get professional help
If loneliness is making you feel low, hopeless or anxious for a long time, talking to a counsellor or doctor can help you work through it.

## How people often put it
People often say things like: I feel so alone, I have no friends, I have no one to talk to, nobody understands me, I spend every weekend by myself, I moved to a new city and don't know anyone, everyone has their own group except me, I feel left out, I feel invisible, I feel lonely even around people.
