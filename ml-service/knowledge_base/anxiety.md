# Anxiety and generalized anxiety disorder
Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anxiety-disorders
Source: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders

## Overview
Anxiety is a normal response to stress or danger, but in an anxiety disorder the worry or fear is much stronger than the situation calls for, is hard to control, and keeps coming back. In generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) a person worries excessively about many everyday things, such as health, money, work, studies or family, on most days for several months.

## How it can feel
People describe a constant feeling that something bad is about to happen, a racing mind that will not switch off, or being unable to stop worrying and overthinking. They may feel on edge, nervous, tense or restless, find it hard to relax, and lie awake at night going over worries.

## Common signs
Excessive worry that is hard to control; restlessness or feeling keyed up; getting tired easily; difficulty concentrating or the mind going blank; irritability; muscle tension, headaches or stomach problems; trouble falling or staying asleep.

## What can help
Slow breathing, for example breathing in for four counts and out for six. Regular physical activity. Cutting down on caffeine and alcohol. Setting aside a short "worry time" each day instead of worrying all day. Writing worries down and separating what can be acted on from what cannot. Grounding techniques such as naming things you can see, hear and feel around you.

## When to get professional help
If worry is present most days, feels out of control, or is getting in the way of sleep, work, studies or relationships, speak to a doctor or mental health professional. CBT is one of the most effective treatments for anxiety, and medication can help some people.
