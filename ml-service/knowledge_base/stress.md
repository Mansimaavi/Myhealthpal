# Stress
Source: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/so-stressed-out-fact-sheet
Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/stress

## Overview
Stress is the body's reaction to pressure or a challenge, such as exams, deadlines, money problems, relationship difficulties or caring for someone. Short-term stress can help us focus, but stress that goes on for a long time can affect both mental and physical health.

## How it can feel
People describe feeling overwhelmed, under pressure, stretched too thin, unable to switch off, or like there is too much to do and not enough time. Students often describe exam stress, fear of failing or pressure from family expectations. Stress can make people short-tempered, tearful or unable to enjoy anything.

## Common signs
Irritability; feeling overwhelmed; racing thoughts; difficulty concentrating; headaches, muscle tension or stomach upset; sleeping badly; eating more or less than usual; relying more on caffeine, alcohol or smoking; withdrawing from people.

## What can help
Breaking large tasks into smaller steps and focusing on one at a time. Making a realistic plan and saying no to extra commitments where possible. Regular sleep, movement and meals. Short breaks during study or work. Talking about what is causing the stress with someone you trust. Relaxation techniques such as slow breathing, stretching or mindfulness.

## When to get professional help
If stress has lasted for weeks, is affecting your sleep, health, work or studies, or you are struggling to cope, talk to a doctor or counsellor. Long-term stress can lead to anxiety or depression, and getting support early helps.

## How people often put it
People often say things like: I'm overwhelmed, I have too much on my plate, I'm under so much pressure, deadlines and assignments are piling up, I'm drowning in work, my exams are coming and I'm not ready, my parents expect too much from me, I'm stretched too thin, I can't keep up, I'm always rushing.
