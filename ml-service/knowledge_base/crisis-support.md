# Crisis support, self-harm and suicidal thoughts
Source: https://telemanas.mohfw.gov.in
Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/suicide

## Overview
Some people go through moments where they feel they cannot go on, think about ending their life, or want to hurt themselves. These feelings can be overwhelming, but they can pass, and support is available. Talking about suicide does not put the idea into someone's head, and reaching out is a sign of strength.

## How it can feel
People describe feeling hopeless, trapped, like a burden to others, like there is no way out, wanting the pain to stop, or wanting to disappear. Some people feel numb, and some feel a sudden calm after deciding to act.

## Warning signs
Talking about wanting to die or not wanting to be here anymore; feeling hopeless or trapped; self-harming; giving away belongings or saying goodbye; withdrawing from others; increased use of alcohol or drugs; searching for ways to end one's life.

## Getting help now in India
Tele-MANAS is the Government of India's free, confidential mental health helpline. It is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, in many languages, on 14416 or 1-800-891-4416. In an emergency or if someone is in immediate danger, call 112 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

## What can help right now
Telling someone you trust how you are feeling. Staying with other people or in a public place instead of being alone. Moving away from anything you could use to hurt yourself. Focusing on getting through the next few minutes or hours rather than the future. Contacting a helpline or a doctor.

## How people often put it
People often say things like: I want to die, I want to end my life, I keep thinking about ending it, I can't go on anymore, I don't want to be here, everyone would be better off without me, I want to hurt myself, I've been cutting myself, I feel like there's no way out, I just want the pain to stop.
