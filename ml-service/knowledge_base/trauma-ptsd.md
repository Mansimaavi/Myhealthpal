# Trauma and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
Source: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd

## Overview
After a frightening or dangerous event, such as an accident, assault, abuse, violence or a disaster, it is normal to feel shaken for some time. PTSD is when strong reactions continue for more than a month and interfere with daily life.

## How it can feel
People describe flashbacks or nightmares that make them feel like the event is happening again, feeling jumpy or constantly on guard, avoiding anything that reminds them of what happened, feeling numb or detached, and blaming themselves.

## Common signs
Intrusive memories, flashbacks or nightmares; avoiding reminders of the event; feeling on edge, easily startled, or having trouble sleeping; negative thoughts about oneself or the world; feeling emotionally numb or cut off from others.

## What can help
Reminding yourself that these are common reactions to an abnormal event. Staying connected with people you trust. Keeping routines. Grounding techniques when memories feel overwhelming, such as focusing on what you can see and touch right now. Avoiding alcohol and drugs as a way of coping.

## When to get professional help
If distressing reactions last more than a month or are getting worse, see a doctor or mental health professional. Trauma-focused therapies such as trauma-focused CBT and EMDR are effective. If you are not safe where you are, seek help from emergency services.

## How people often put it
People often say things like: I keep having flashbacks, I have nightmares about what happened, since the accident or assault I feel on edge, I'm jumpy and startle easily, I avoid places that remind me of it, I feel numb and disconnected, I can't stop reliving it, I don't feel safe anymore, it happens again in my head.
